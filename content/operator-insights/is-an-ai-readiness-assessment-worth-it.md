**An AI readiness assessment is worth paying for when you're about to spend real money on AI, you don't yet know where it will pay off, and someone in your company will act on what it finds. If you already know the problem, or nobody owns the outcome, skip it and spend the money elsewhere.**

- A good assessment tells you what to fix first, what it will take, and what to leave alone.
- A weak one tells you what you already believed, in 60 slides.
- Free online assessments are useful for a gut check, but they're self-reported and most are built to start a sales conversation.
- Our AI Readiness Assessment costs $15,000 to $30,000, takes 3 to 5 weeks, and covers one company.
- About half of the reasons not to buy one are below. Read them first.

Here's the real issue: most AI projects don't fail because the model was wrong. They fail because the data, processes and systems underneath weren't ready. Gartner predicts that through 2026, organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects that aren't supported by AI-ready data, and 63% of organizations say they either lack the right data practices for AI or aren't sure they have them ([Gartner, February 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk)). An assessment is how you find that out before you've paid for a build, not after.

## What do you actually get for $15,000 to $30,000?

Three things you can use, delivered at the end of 3 to 5 weeks:

1. **A detailed written report.** Your readiness scored across six areas (Data Integrity, Process Documentation, Systems Architecture, Team Alignment, Measurement Framework and AI Adoption Readiness), built up from 12 underlying dimensions. Each one is scored twice: once for how capable you are today, and once for how much return AI could realistically produce there.
2. **A live walkthrough with your leadership team.** A slide deck that summarizes the report, presented in a working session so decisions get made in the room.
3. **A prioritized roadmap.** What to fix first, what comes next and what to leave alone, with an effort estimate and an indicative cost band on every item.

You'll also see where you land on a five-level scale (Not Ready, Emerging, Developing, Scalable, AI-Forward), and which of four profiles fits you:

- Strong foundations, clear opportunities: you're ready to scale what works.
- Strong foundations, unclear opportunities: you have the tools but aren't pointing them at problems that pay.
- Weak foundations, clear opportunities: the payoff is real, but the data and processes need work first.
- Weak foundations, unclear opportunities: AI isn't the next investment yet.

That profile does more work than the score. It decides whether your first dollar goes into building, fixing, or neither.

## When is an AI readiness assessment worth it?

- You're planning meaningful AI spend in the next six to twelve months and don't know where it should go first.
- Earlier AI or automation projects stalled and nobody agrees on why.
- Leadership is split on priorities and needs one evidence-based answer to decide from.
- You need a plan you can defend to a board or investors, with costs attached.
- One executive owns the outcome and has budget to act on what comes back.

## When should you skip it?

## Should you trust a firm that credits the fee toward its own work?

You should be skeptical, and the skepticism is reasonable. Our Assessment earns a $5,000 credit toward a retainer if one starts within 30 days, and any firm that credits its diagnostic toward its own build has an incentive to find work for itself.

How to check us: read the roadmap for items your own team can do without us; a good one has them. Look for things it tells you not to do yet. And hold us to the line we work by: "If we can't build it and prove its impact, we don't recommend it." The roadmap is yours either way. You can hand it to your own team or to another firm.

## What does a good assessment look like?

| A good assessment | A weak one |
|---|---|
| Scores based on evidence from your systems and data | Scores based on what people said in interviews |
| A roadmap with effort and cost on each item | A vision statement and a list of possibilities |
| Tells you what not to do yet | Recommends everything |
| Names work your team can do themselves | Routes every item back to the firm that wrote it |
| Short enough to act on | 60 slides restating your own interviews |
| Tells you something you didn't already believe | Confirms what you walked in thinking |

Use this table on any firm you're considering, including us.

Most teams get this wrong: they buy the assessment to feel ready, not to decide. If you won't change what you spend money on based on what it finds, it isn't worth $15,000, or $1,500.
