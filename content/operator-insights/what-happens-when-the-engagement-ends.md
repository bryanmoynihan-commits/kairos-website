**Yes, as long as one person on your side owns what we built. Everything lives in your systems, under your accounts, written down and trained into your team. When we leave, you keep all of it.**

- Every build ships with an SOP, documentation, recorded walkthroughs and training.
- We agree on your internal owner at kickoff, so the handoff goes to a person, not a folder.
- Under our standard Master Services Agreement (MSA), you own the work we build for you once it's paid for.
- We deactivate our access and rotate shared credentials as part of the handoff.
- If you'd rather we keep looking after things, there's a smaller maintenance retainer for that.

The real risk isn't the consultant leaving. It's nobody inside your company picking it up. That's the part we plan for from the first week.

## What do you keep when the engagement ends?

| What | Where it lives | Who owns it |
|---|---|---|
| Automations, workflows and AI tools we built | Your systems, under your accounts | You |
| Data your new systems and automations create: records, logs, enriched fields, AI outputs | Your systems | You |
| Configuration changes to your CRM and other tools | Your systems | You |
| SOPs, documentation and recorded walkthroughs | Your shared drive or knowledge base | You |
| Accounts for any new tools we set up | Your company's name, on your billing | You |
| Reports, roadmaps and other deliverables | Wherever you keep them | You |

Nothing runs on a platform we control, and nothing stops working because our contract ended.

## What does the handoff include?

- **An SOP and documentation for every build:** what it does, how it works and what to check when it misbehaves.
- **Recorded walkthroughs** your team keeps and can rewatch when someone new joins.
- **Training for the people who use each build,** plus deeper training for whoever will maintain it.
- **A named internal owner,** agreed at kickoff. Usually it's the day-to-day technical lead who worked alongside us throughout.
- **A short list of what will need attention:** the models, vendor connections and fields most likely to change, and who should watch each one.
- **A clean exit on access:** our accounts deactivated, shared credentials rotated, and anything we set up confirmed as yours.

## Do you actually own what we build?

Under our standard Master Services Agreement (MSA), yes. Once the work is paid for, the work product we create for you is assigned to you, and your data, including everything your new systems generate, stays yours.

Two honest caveats. First, some builds use reusable components we bring to every client, such as templates, code libraries and workflow patterns. Those stay ours, but the MSA gives you a permanent, royalty-free license to use them as built inside your business. Second, we keep the general know-how and methods we refine along the way, so we can use what we learn on future work. That never includes your confidential information. In practice, nothing you rely on can be taken away, and nothing confidential leaves with us.

## What will still need attention after we leave?

AI and automation systems aren't build-once-and-forget. Model versions change. Vendors update their connections. Someone renames a field in the CRM. None of that breaks a well-documented system overnight, but someone has to notice and adjust.

That's why the handoff includes a list of what's most likely to change and who should watch it. For most builds, an internal owner with the documentation and a few hours a month can keep things running.

## What if something breaks after the engagement ends?

Under the MSA, if something we delivered doesn't meet the agreed standard and you tell us within 30 days, we redo it at no charge. After that, you have two options:

- **Your team handles it,** using the SOPs and recordings.
- **We keep looking after it** through a maintenance retainer, scaled down from our standard retainers and scoped to the systems you want us to watch. That can include what we built and, if it helps, other systems around it.

## Why would a firm that sells retainers build you out of needing it?

Because a retainer should continue only when there's more worth building. If the only reason to keep paying us is that something stops working without us, the handoff failed. We'd rather earn the next engagement than make it necessary.

## When won't this stick?

When nobody owns it. Here's the pattern we keep seeing: companies that name an internal owner early and keep that person in the room keep their gains. Companies that treat the handoff as a last-week task watch the improvements fade within a quarter.

If you can't name someone who'll own what gets built, sort that out before you start. We'll help you work out who it should be.
